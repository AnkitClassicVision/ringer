# Vintage Optical One-Page Messaging Notes

## Purpose

These are two internal presentations of the same accepted Vintage Optical evidence.

- `current-onepager.html` retains the approved bottom-safe report word for word and replaces only the drawn brand symbol with the approved MyBCAT logo.
- `template-framed-onepager.html` adds a stronger executive narrative, visual hierarchy, ranked proof-to-unlock tests, a First 30 Days strip, and an internal decision call to action.

The template-framed version is additive framing over the same accepted evidence. It is not a new forecast, score interpretation, revenue estimate, or market conclusion.

## What the uploaded sample contributed

The sample contributed presentation structure, not facts:

- A dark hero with a large, outcome-oriented headline and circular score gauge.
- A short starred executive read immediately below the hero.
- Three numbered zones that move from market to competition to opportunity.
- Large proof points that can be understood in seconds.
- A competition wedge that states the narrowest useful takeaway.
- Ranked opportunity rows with badges, meters, and a specific proof needed before action.
- A compact three-move First 30 Days strip.
- A visible call to action followed by methodology and limitation language.

Those devices make the accepted evidence easier to scan. The Vintage version changes the sample's action label from `WIN MORE` to `MEASURE FIRST`, because the approved score is directional public-only evidence and the key constraints remain unresolved.

## Messaging differences

### Retained version

The retained report is the audit-friendly evidence page. It preserves the complete five-window catchment table, corrected route evidence, all approved score meters, three specialty research lanes, explicit unknowns, and the original three Fix Cards. Its strength is full traceability inside a fixed one-page layout.

### Template-framed version

The template-framed report is the decision-facing summary. It leads with the tension `Market support is real. Proof is the bottleneck.` It uses four market proof points, bounds the review comparison to one same-page Google sample, and converts the evidence gaps into four ranked tests. The decision request is limited to approving a 30-day measurement sprint.

Neither version claims that the market result is a grade, that the review sample proves patient choice, or that a specialty lane has established demand or economics.

## Tempting sample claims deliberately rejected

The sample's persuasive language was reviewed claim by claim. The following claims or implications were not carried into the Vintage report:

1. The sample's 74-point opportunity score, `WIN MORE` label, and dominate, vulnerable, or at-risk bands.
2. The claim that demand is not the practice's problem.
3. The claim that the service-area profile is ideal.
4. Unsupported household-income, children, senior, and work-from-home percentages.
5. The implication that demographic context proves premium optical, cash-pay care, family exam demand, specialty demand, or weekday visit behavior.
6. The claim that competitors are weak where it matters.
7. The claim that four revenue lanes are open.
8. The claim that a tight market with open lanes is where growth comes from.
9. The sample's location-specific Room to Win scores and anchor-office or growth-office labels.
10. The sample's Tier 1 and Tier 2 competitor classification as a complete market structure.
11. The claim that competitors win patients on convenience rather than care.
12. Claims about competitor scheduling problems, long waits, billing confusion, or frictionless booking.
13. The claim that Vintage review content proves a care-quality or communication advantage.
14. The claim that a review gap is revenue that can be won.
15. Appointment-fill and recall-recapture volume assumptions.
16. Average-visit values, weekly recovery examples, annualized calculations, and all revenue ranges.
17. The claim that senior or screen-time context proves dry-eye demand.
18. The claim that no local competitor owns a specialty lane.
19. The claim that child share and parent affluence prove myopia-management demand.
20. The claim that specialty contact lenses create a moat that retail competitors cannot copy.
21. The sample's lane-readiness labels such as `Own the lane`, `Fastest lever`, and `Build + test` when those labels imply proven demand, capacity, or economics.
22. Any combined directional upside.
23. The claim that each office should own distinct service lines.
24. The proposed campaign language positioned against competitor pain.
25. Any external strategy-call invitation, membership promotion, outbound link, or marketing promise.
26. Any implication that public evidence establishes canonical competitor share, a complete competitor set, patient choice, cross-platform review totals, live access conditions, conversion, capacity, outcomes, ownership, or service-line economics.
27. Any statement that growth is guaranteed or that observed population and enrollment changes are forecasts.

## Approved framing boundary

The new variant may say that modeled catchments and observed local growth support further measurement, that competitive pressure remains meaningful, and that Vintage leads review volume in one bounded same-page Google sample. It may recommend three controlled measurements and rank four proof-to-unlock tests.

It may not turn those facts into market share, patient-choice, demand, capacity, economics, revenue, ownership, or guaranteed-growth claims. The internal decision is whether to authorize measurement, not whether to authorize broad spend or an external campaign.
