# Vintage Optical One-Pager Research

## The Read

Vintage Optical looks publicly competitive, but the Morton market cannot be scored with peer-level precision from this evidence set. The practice has three listed optometrists, a modernized location, an on-site lab, online first-available booking, specific specialty pathways, and favorable placement in a seven-query search sample. [E002, E003, E004, E005, E006, E007, E008, E012, E013, E014, E015, E016, E017, E018, E020] Those are verified public strengths. The sampled rankings are not a local rank grid, and the evidence does not establish booking conversion, capacity, patient preference, or clinical outcomes.

The score stack reconciles as follows. Market Demand-Supply is **59/100, Mixed** because Morton has useful income and age-demand proxies, but no complete supply denominator, growth measure, payer evidence, or drive-time catchment. [E029, E030, E037] Competitive Pressure is **56/100, High pressure at the lower edge**, reflecting visible nearby and regional alternatives while avoiding an unsupported office-density or patient-choice calculation. [E021, E022, E023, E024, E025, E026, E027, E037] Room to Win is therefore **44/100, higher is better**, exactly 100 minus pressure. Practice Competitiveness is **72/100, Competitive**, led by visibility, bookability, specialty specificity, and professional authority. [E002, E003, E004, E005, E009, E010, E012, E013, E014, E015, E016, E017, E018, E019, E020] Client Opportunity is **66/100, higher is better**, meaning there is actionable upside, but it should begin with small measurement tests rather than a claim of missed demand.

Confidence is **C**. Scores use the rubric’s qualitative-band allowance because comparable peer percentiles are incomplete. They are decision aids, not measured demand, market share, or performance.

## Score Rationale

**Market Demand-Supply, 59/100, higher is better.** Verified: the population, household-income, child, and senior figures provide demand-fit context. [E029, E030] Inference: family/myopia and senior/medical or dry-eye demand are plausible hypotheses. They are not proof of service demand, willingness to pay, or payer fit. Supply Balance and Growth remain cautious because the ledger lacks a deduplicated provider count, Vision Demand Units, trend series, and drive-time catchment.

**Competitive Pressure, 56/100, higher means more pressure.** Verified: Focus On Eyes offers broad local care and Saturday hours; Tri-County and Vision Care Center show specialty overlap; Bard offers retail and care breadth; Illinois Eye Center offers a medical/surgical continuum. [E021, E022, E023, E024, E025, E026, E027] Approximate straight-line bands place several alternatives within 8.5 miles, but these are not travel times. [E037] Inference: alternatives create meaningful pressure. The score stays near the lower edge of High because reachable choice counts and competitor reputation strength are unknown.

**Room to Win, 44/100, higher is better.** This is formula-derived as 100 minus 56. It says the public market looks relatively tight. It does not say Vintage is losing patients.

**Practice Competitiveness, 72/100, higher is better.** Verified: specific specialty pages, an online booking path, sound crawl hygiene, sampled search visibility, and independent contact-lens authority are present. [E002, E003, E004, E005, E009, E010, E012, E013, E014, E015, E016, E017, E018, E019, E020] Inference: Vintage has a strong public foundation and a credible specialty story. A Market Leader label would overreach because comparable review volume, booking friction, location convenience, and rank-grid evidence are missing.

**Client Opportunity, 66/100, higher is better.** Verified: Vintage already owns visible assets worth measuring rather than replacing. [E002, E003, E004, E005, E009, E010, E020] Inference: the easiest upside is diagnostic and operational: identify which specialty funnel converts, whether access gaps exist, and whether trust loops repeat. [E033, E034, E035]

## Opportunity Lanes

1. **Specialty contact lenses, 72/100, Build/Test.** Vintage has specific capability and independent professional authority. [E004, E009, E010] The least-known pieces are inquiries, fits, capacity, continuation, referral sources, and whether the workflow transfers across the team.
2. **Dry eye, 68/100, Build/Test.** Vintage’s modalities are specific, and Morton’s senior share supports a demand hypothesis. [E002, E030] Tri-County and Vision Care Center show visible overlap, so this is not proven white space. [E023, E024]
3. **Myopia management, 67/100, Build/Test.** Vintage offers four approaches, and Morton has a sizable under-18 share. [E003, E030] Vision Care Center offers similar modalities. [E025] The age segment is context, not measured demand.
4. **Access and trust systems, qualitative operating lane.** Online booking, sampled visibility, and AOA authority already exist. [E009, E010, E012, E013, E014, E015, E016, E017, E018, E020] The open question is whether these assets consistently produce completed appointments, referrals, reviews, recalls, and optical follow-through.

## Three Actions

1. **Baseline one specialty funnel for four weeks.** Choose dry eye, myopia, specialty contacts, or Neurolens. Count aggregate qualified inquiries, consults booked, consults completed, and starts by source, with no patient-identifying fields. Proof is a complete weekly funnel that locates a drop-off or shows none. Stop if conversion and capacity already meet thresholds set by Vintage. [E002, E003, E004, E005, E033]
2. **Run a four-week access diagnostic.** Aggregate appointment-intent contacts by weekday and hour, then record answered, booked, and first-available outcomes. Proof is a visible recurring gap by time or service, or evidence that no meaningful gap exists. Do not change hours or staffing without that pattern. [E020, E022, E034]
3. **Audit one trust loop and address consistency.** Track one aggregate recall, referral, review-request, or optical-follow-through loop for four weeks. Separately check whether the live result showing the former address is editable; the current site is authoritative. [E031, E032] Proof is one end-to-end baseline plus corrected editable high-visibility listings. Stop before building a new campaign if both are already healthy. [E009, E010, E035]

## Disconfirmers

- The seven sampled first-place results may not reproduce across devices, locations, dates, or a formal rank grid. [E012, E013, E014, E015, E016, E017, E018]
- Focus On Eyes may capture more routine demand than its public pages reveal because of tenure and published access. [E021, E022]
- Vision Care Center may have a stronger specialty position than this scan captures. [E024, E025]
- Vintage’s visible capabilities may not have spare capacity or reliable conversion.
- Professional authority may be concentrated in one clinician rather than embedded in team workflows. [E006, E009, E010]
- Straight-line distances may materially misstate practical choice. [E037]

## Missing Data

The public record does not establish true patient volume, actual payer mix, comparable review volume, drive-time patient-choice counts, capacity, conversion, profitability, leakage, or owner dependence. Confidence would improve with a deduplicated provider inventory and drive-time model; a comparable review corpus; a formal rank grid and authorized GBP/GSC data; aggregate call, booking, recall, referral, optical, and specialty-funnel summaries; and service-line capacity and financial history. None of those unknowns should be treated as zero.

## Decision Residue

**Hardest decision:** whether numeric client-safe scores were defensible without peer percentiles. The decision was to use conservative qualitative rubric bands, show direction, separate verified facts from inference, and cap confidence at C.

**Alternatives rejected:** reusing the longer report without the canonical score stack; calling visible specialty services proven white space; labeling sampled search placement a stable local ranking; converting demographic or operational hypotheses into dollar forecasts; and recommending longer hours, new services, or broad paid awareness before measurement.

**Least-confident assumption:** the reviewed public competitor set and straight-line bands are sufficient to place competitive pressure at the lower edge of High. A drive-time, provider-deduplication, and reputation pass could move that score materially.
